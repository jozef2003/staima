# How communication/marketing agencies use AI (2025–2026) and which workflows are worth automating — focus DACH / MyTy-type agency groups

Context: MYTY Group AG is a network of specialised agencies (Strichpunkt, SiR MaRY, Monami, 404, SESAMY, MOONGENCY, VYSION, norisk, SHAPE, CRK, Agence Trio) covering Brand & Identity, Communication & Experience, Performance/Growth & Media, eCommerce/Digital Products & AI, and Reputation & Public Affairs; Berlin hub "The HYVE by MYTY" — [MYTY Group](https://www.myty.com/de/)

## 1. Which agency tasks use the most time / where AI is already used (by process step)

### Takeaway
Almost every German agency now uses GenAI (98 % in the BVDW/Observatory study of 201 agencies), mostly for ideation, text, translation, research and data analysis. Operations work (project management, briefing management, QA) is already 65 % penetrated. Campaign activation and media are only at 45 %. For PR specifically, media relations is the core job (84 %), followed by thought leadership, measurement/reporting (44 %) and content creation. The biggest measurable waste is in approvals and feedback loops, and in reporting.

### Cited Findings
**BVDW x Observatory "Treiber der Transformation" (survey 20.01.–17.02.2025, n=201 German agencies; 33 % full-service, 30 % digital, 16 % creative, 12 % PR, 6 % social media)** — [BVDW PDF](https://www.bvdw.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/BVDW-x-Observatory-KI-Agenturbefragung.pdf)
- 98 % use generative AI; 79 % have used it for 1–3 years, 13 % for more than 3 years.
- Strategy: 81 % use GenAI in strategic planning. Research 83 %, brand/comms strategy 76 %, market insights 66 %, personas 62 %, customer insights 59 %, customer journey 47 %, channel planning 33 %.
- Creative: 95 % use it. Ideation 88 %, visual design 67 %, mock-ups 66 %, storyboards 55 %, testing ideas 50 %, corporate design 28 %, CX 15 %.
- Production: 76 % use it. Text 88 %, translation 72 %, design 65 %, voiceover 58 %, video 56 %, asset production 56 %, subtitles 48 %, localisation/adaptation 44 %, audio 44 %, asset management 28 %, 3D 25 %, avatars 21 %.
- Campaign activation/media: only 45 % use it. Within that group: social media 79 %, SEO 55 %, targeting 50 %, campaign optimisation 49 %, campaign management 47 %, media planning 34 %, distribution 26 %, media buying 12 %.
- Data analysis: 66 % use it. Analysis 94 %, visualisation 55 %, forecasting models 47 %.
- Operations/PM: 66 % use it. Project management 75 %, briefing/requirements management 66 %, QA 46 %, testing 37 %, capacity/resource planning 25 %.
- 29 % have built their own AI models and 54 % customised models (70 % among agencies with more than 50 employees; only 13 % of the larger agencies have neither). Most of these models run in creative work (60 %), production (52 %), strategy (44 %) and operations/PM (40 %).
- 69 % plan to increase GenAI investment in 2025. 86 % report no change in headcount, 9 % grew, 5 % shrank.
- Client demand: at 62 % of agencies, fewer than 10 % of clients explicitly commission AI projects. Yet most of the agencies' internal AI use is on client projects.
- New billing and business models are emerging, including licence, subscription and SaaS models for agencies' own tools.

**Muck Rack State of PR 2025 (n=1,089 surveyed / 911 usable, June–July 2025, 51 % agencies, 90 % US)** — [Muck Rack PDF](https://media.muckrack.com/documents/State_of_PR_2025_report.pdf)
- Activities that make up at least 25 % of the job: media relations 84 %, thought leadership 45 %, media measurement & reporting 44 %, content creation 42 %, internal comms 30 %, corporate comms 24 %, crisis 21 %, events 21 %, influencer relations 12 %.
- 77 % already use GenAI and 11 % plan to. 59 % say AI/automation will gain most in importance over the next 5 years (the top answer).
- 67 % say "producing measurable results" is the best way to raise PR's perceived value. This points to reporting and measurement as a high-value workflow.
- Earned media is getting harder: 72 % cite low journalist response rates, 62 % shrinking media lists, 49 % more competition, 42 % an overloaded or understaffed team, 21 % difficulty personalising pitches at scale.
- 25 % pitch more than 50 journalists per announcement. 75 % of those who personalise change only "a few sentences". Most pitch reporters they have barely worked with before (36 % say "a few of them").
- 53 % use no social listening tool. LinkedIn is the most valuable platform (56 %, up from 50 %), and 39 % stopped using X professionally.
- Muck Rack's AI-specific study: 98 % edit AI output to keep brand voice. 51 % now work somewhere with an AI policy (up from 21 % in 2024) and 43 % get AI training (up from 21 %). AI is used for brainstorming (more than 80 %), social copy (64 %), press releases (58 %) and pitches (54 %) — [PRSA summary](https://www.prsa.org/article/state-of-public-relations-ai-media-relations-SEPT25); [PR News, 5 takeaways](https://www.prnewsonline.com/5-key-takeaways-from-muck-racks-state-of-ai-in-pr-report/). These figures are summarised from secondary write-ups, not checked against the primary AI report.

**news aktuell / PER PR-Trendmonitor (DE+CH, n=327 comms professionals in companies, organisations and PR agencies, Feb 2024)** — [Horizont](https://www.horizont.net/schweiz/nachrichten/trendmonitor-wie-pr-spezialisten-in-der-schweiz-und-deutschland-den-einsatz-von-kibewerten-220229); [news aktuell / Tagesspiegel OTS](https://www.tagesspiegel.de/advertorials/ots/ki-freie-zonen-wo-pr-profis-bewusst-keine-kunstliche-intelligenz-einsetzen-15590958.html)
- AI use: translation 43 %, text creation and editing 33 %, topic research 16 %, image recognition 8 %, video/audio 4 %. Text creation, topic/trend finding and media monitoring are named as the main uses.
- "AI-free zones": 73 % would not hand crisis prevention or crisis management to AI, 68 % reputation management, 67 % influencer relations. Legal content and sensitive topics are also avoided.
- Concerns: lack of verifiability and control of information (51 %), unclear legal and copyright situation (34 %). 41 % in DE/CH did not yet use AI at work at that time. This is 2024 data and probably outdated given the 98 % agency figure from 2025.

**IAB (Institut für Arbeitsmarkt- und Berufsforschung) business panel (approx. 15,000 businesses)**: the communication sector leads GenAI adoption at 59 %, against 25 % of all German firms in 2025 (5 % in 2023). 90 % of adopting firms use freely available software, 16 % bought solutions trained on their own data, 6 % built their own — [it-daily](https://www.it-daily.net/?p=175780)

**Approvals / feedback loops**
- 80 % of account managers got delayed client feedback in the past year. Filestage claims a dedicated proofing tool saves about one day per person per month (vendor claim) — [Filestage](https://filestage.io/blog/client-proofing-software/)
- Brand-side marketers and creatives say it takes about 10 days on average to get a piece of work approved, and waiting for feedback and chasing approvals are the top two causes of creative delay — [MarketingProfs 2023](https://www.marketingprofs.com/charts/2023/48820/what-slows-the-creative-process-down)
- Only about a third of agencies think they receive constructive client feedback (IPA study) — [Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/constructive-feedback-ipa/)

**Reporting**
- 49 % of agency teams spend 1–3 hours preparing each client reporting meeting, per client (Databox survey, cited via secondary source) — [Databox](https://databox.com/?p=190464); [AgencyAnalytics benchmarks](https://agencyanalytics.com/blog/marketing-agency-benchmarks-client-reporting-trends)
- Claims of "137 billable hours per month wasted on reports", "75 % less report time (Forrester)" and "3–6 h of monitoring saved per account per week" come from vendor or blog sources ([dev.to](https://dev.to/alessandrobinda114/marketing-agencies-waste-137-billable-hours-per-month-on-client-reports-29g2), [lowcode.agency](https://www.lowcode.agency/blog/ai-employee-for-pr-firms)). They are low reliability and should not be quoted as fact.

### Inferences
- The most valuable workflows to automate (pain × frequency × AI-readiness), ranked for a MyTy-type group:
  1. **Media monitoring → daily press review (Pressespiegel) → monthly client report with narrative.** 44 % of PR pros spend at least 25 % of their time on measurement, and 67 % see measurable results as the main value lever.
  2. **Pitch personalisation and media-list research.** Response rates are low, and 75 % change only a few sentences, so personalisation at scale is an obvious gap.
  3. **Briefing intake → structured brief → task breakdown.** 66 % of AI-using agencies already use it for briefing management.
  4. **Client approval loops** (comment collection, version summaries, reminders). This is the largest "waiting" waste, and few AI tools address it.
  5. **Content adaptation, translation and localisation.** 72 % already use AI for translation, and in DACH there is constant DE/EN/FR work (MyTy owns the French Agence Trio).
  6. **New-business/RFP research and pitch decks.**
  7. **Social content calendars and copy.**
  8. **Influencer search, vetting and briefing.**
- Capacity planning (25 %) and campaign activation (45 %) are low-penetration areas, so there is less competition there, but also less tested demand.

### Gaps
- No German study quantifying hours per task (e.g. hours per week on press reviews or reports) was found. The hour figures above are US or vendor-sourced.
- The GPRA has published no dedicated AI usage survey that I could find (the search returned only GPRA restructuring news, [Horizont](https://www.horizont.net/agenturen/nachrichten/-GPRA-Agenturen-richten-sich-neu-aus-99944)).

## 2. Which AI tools agencies use for each workflow, and the gaps/complaints

### Takeaway
Agencies stack general LLMs (ChatGPT/Copilot, plus DeepL for translation) on top of specialised SaaS: Meltwater/Talkwalker/Muck Rack/Cision for monitoring and media, Modash/HypeAuditor/CreatorIQ/Klear for influencers, Sprout/Agorapulse/Hootsuite for social, Filestage-type tools for approvals. The holding groups (WPP, Publicis, Omnicom) build their own "agentic operating layers". Mid-sized groups like MyTy cannot build these, which leaves an opening for a platform like Claudia. The main complaints are: output needs heavy editing (98 % edit), constant switching between tools, unverifiable facts, and data protection.

### Cited Findings
- **Holding-company AI platforms:** Publicis Marcel (since 2018; agentic campaigns), WPP Open (2024; agentic hub with privacy-safe data) and Omnicom Omni (updated 2025; agentic orchestration) converge on the same features: agents, audience data, in-flight optimisation and "how the brand appears in LLMs" — [The Media Leader](https://uk.themedialeader.com/agency-groups-ai-platforms-explained/)
- WPP Agent Hub is an internal "app store" that turns WPP's data, frameworks and methods into specialised agents — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/inside-wpps-new-ai-play-turning-agency-know-how-into-digital-agents-87198.htm). WPP reportedly runs about 50,000 AI agents and relies less on freelancers — [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/advertising-news/wpp-reduces-reliance-on-freelancers-deploys-50000-ai-agents-to-boost-efficiency-146192.html). WPP Open Pro is self-serve for brands, i.e. a potential substitute for agencies — [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2025/10/24/can-wpp-s-self-serve-ai-offer-open-pro-win-without-cannibalizing-the-agency-market); [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/wpps-new-ai-platform-offers-marketing-without-agencies-for-smbs/803723/)
- At CES 2026, agentic AI became the agencies' "operating layer": AI sits on top of existing tools rather than replacing them, linking insight to action and standardising workflows — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/agentic-ai-becomes-agencies--operating-layer-ces-2026)
- **Media monitoring:** Meltwater "Mira" AI teammate with Mira Projects and Mira Canvas; predictive spike forecasting; "GenAI Lens" (how brands appear in LLMs); Klear AI for influencer discovery with natural-language search and personalised outreach (Oct 2025 release) — [Meltwater press release](https://www.meltwater.com/en/press-releases/meltwater-2025-year-end-product-release-delivers-smarter-media-intelligence-with-new-ai-innovations). Meltwater markets AI for agencies' RFP/pitch preparation — [Meltwater blog](https://meltwater.com/en/blog/mira-for-pr-agencies)
- Muck Rack positions itself as an all-in-one AI PR platform: media database, monitoring, reporting and pitching — [Muck Rack report](https://media.muckrack.com/documents/State_of_PR_2025_report.pdf)
- **Influencer:** Modash (250M+ profiles, from $199/month, natural-language search), HypeAuditor (fraud detection; "94 % accuracy" is a vendor/blog claim), CreatorIQ (enterprise, from about $35K/year) — [Syncly comparison (vendor blog)](https://syncly.app/blog/best-influencer-discovery-tools-agencies); [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/influencer-discovery-tools). Pain points: filtering creators, fraud/audience validation, and the "activation bottleneck" of moving from discovery to execution without switching tools — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/?p=221865). In the 2026 Benchmark Report, 72 % of brands plan to grow budgets by more than half — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/?p=221865). Creator discovery is called the top AI use case (about 36.7 %), and about 19.4 % of brands outsource discovery and vetting to agencies (single source, [presenc.ai](https://presenc.ai/research/influencer-discovery-ai-platforms-2026), not verified).
- **European/DACH tools:** EU vendors (Mistral, Aleph Alpha, Neuroflash) are gaining in the Mittelstand. Many CFOs rule out production use without a data processing agreement (AVV) and EU hosting (blog source, medium reliability) — [collectivebrain.de](https://collectivebrain.de/top-12-ki-tools/)
- **Complaints:** 98 % edit AI output and 61 % must edit most of it ([PR News](https://www.prnewsonline.com/5-key-takeaways-from-muck-racks-state-of-ai-in-pr-report/)). 51 % cite lack of verifiability and control ([Horizont Trendmonitor](https://www.horizont.net/schweiz/nachrichten/trendmonitor-wie-pr-spezialisten-in-der-schweiz-und-deutschland-den-einsatz-von-kibewerten-220229)). GWA reports that only 17 % use advanced analytics tools despite 79 % calling themselves AI avant-garde, and 35 % report problems integrating AI into existing workflows — [markt-kom (GWA AI white paper 2025)](https://www.markt-kom.com/en/?p=231533)

### Inferences
- Tools mapped to workflows, and where Claudia could fit:
  - **Press review / monitoring.** Agencies use Meltwater, Talkwalker, Cision, Muck Rack, and in DACH also pressrelations, Landau Media and Argus (not verified here). Gap: client-specific narrative digests plus action items in the client's chat or group space. Claudia could take connector exports or RSS/Google News feeds and post a daily Pressespiegel into the client space.
  - **Press release / pitch drafting.** ChatGPT/Copilot plus Muck Rack/Cision. Gap: per-client brand voice and fact memory, since 98 % edit output. Claudia's per-client agent with its own memory fits directly.
  - **Decks/RFPs.** Gamma, Copilot in PowerPoint, Canva (not separately verified). Gap: research plus agency case database. Claudia's hosted pages could serve as pitch microsites.
  - **Social calendars.** Sprout, Agorapulse, Hootsuite. Gap: generating calendar plus assets and handling approval in one place. Claudia's content studio plus an approval flow could cover this.
  - **Influencer.** Modash, HypeAuditor, CreatorIQ, Klear. Gap: activation (briefing, contracts, content approval, payout). Claudia's creator/affiliate module is adjacent and could be extended to briefings and content approvals.
  - **Approvals.** Filestage, Ziflow. Gap: AI summarisation of feedback rounds and auto-reminders. Clients could approve inside Claudia's group chat or hosted pages.
  - **Reporting.** AgencyAnalytics, Databox, Looker Studio, Meltwater dashboards. Gap: a narrative "what happened and what's next" written for the client.
- For a mid-sized group, the WPP Agent Hub idea ("turn agency know-how into agents") is the template: per-agency agents carrying each sister agency's frameworks, shared across the network.

### Gaps
- No verified data on ChatGPT Enterprise vs Microsoft Copilot penetration in German agencies.
- No reliable 2025–2026 data found on Talkwalker (now part of Hootsuite) AI features, or on Gamma, Jasper and Copy.ai usage among agencies.
- No primary complaint studies on Sprout/Agorapulse AI.

## 3. German-specific aspects: GDPR/DSGVO, client data separation, works councils, association guidance

### Takeaway
German PR associations (DRPR, DPRG, BdKom, GPRA) require human accountability, fact-checking and transparency, and say a label alone is not enough. Data protection is a top-3 barrier (40 % in the GWA study). Only 19 % of agencies have ethical AI guidelines. Employees pasting client data into ChatGPT can be a dismissible offence. Works councils are involved, but a 2024 Hamburg labour court ruling found no co-determination right just for allowing browser-based ChatGPT. The EU AI Act transparency duties (Art. 50) apply. Together these strongly favour tenant-isolated, EU-hosted, logged, per-client AI workspaces.

### Cited Findings
- DPRG, BdKom and GPRA jointly call for responsible AI use. Labelling AI texts alone is not sufficient, fact-checking is required, and responsibility for the accuracy of statements always lies with the sender — [turi2](https://www.turi2.de/aktuell/pr-verbaende-nehmen-kommunikatoren-bei-ki-generierten-texten-in-die-pflicht/) (date not shown in the excerpt). The guidance also covers data-protection compliance for client and third-party data (from a summary of the same source).
- DRPR (Deutscher Rat für Public Relations) extended its AI guideline: no amplification of discriminatory patterns, no AI use to systematically discredit opposing views or manipulate discourse, fully automated use must be critically reviewed, and "strategy remains a human thinking task with ethical responsibility" — [turi2](https://www.turi2.de/?p=4080010); search summary, primary DRPR text not fetched.
- The Austrian PRVA has a detailed AI guide for PR (2023, updated 2024) — [PRVA KI-Leitfaden 2024](https://prva.at/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/KI-Leitfaden-2024.pdf)
- **GWA AI white paper 2025** (member survey plus expert interviews, about 100 pages): only 19 % have ethical AI guidelines and 32 % have clear responsibilities for AI implementations. Top challenges: unclear strategy implementation 68 %, staff skills 45 %, data protection 40 %, budget 38 %, workflow integration 35 % — [markt-kom](https://www.markt-kom.com/en/?p=231533)
- **GWA Frühjahrsmonitor 2026:** 49 % of agencies name "replacement of their services by AI" as a major growth inhibitor (impact 4–6 of 6), against 98 % naming the weak economy. "AI competence" is the client demand rising fastest (61.1 %). Project fees make up about 62 % of gross income (down from 68 %), and only 8.7 % comes from fixed-price models — [GWA Frühjahrsmonitor 2026 PDF](https://www.gwa.de/content/uploads/2026/03/GWA_Fruehjahrsmonitor_2026-1.pdf)
- **Works council:** the Hamburg labour court ruled in January 2024 that there is no co-determination right under § 87(1) BetrVG when employees may use ChatGPT via browser on private accounts. Rules on employee monitoring and data protection still apply. A DPIA (data protection impact assessment, Art. 35 GDPR) may be required before deployment. Unauthorised entry of confidential customer data into ChatGPT can justify a warning or dismissal — [activemind.legal](https://www.activemind.legal/de/guides/urteil-betriebsrat-ki/); [IWW](https://www.iww.de/as/ki-im-arbeitsverhaeltnis-der-zulaessige-einsatz-9040fd5e-ee7f-411e-a6fa-3dac4139cd77)
- **EU AI Act:** the EU Commission has published guidelines on the Art. 50 transparency obligations (labelling AI-generated or manipulated content) — [DATEV Magazin, 28.07.2026](https://www.datev-magazin.de/nachrichten-steuern-recht/recht/transparenzpflichten-nach-art-50-ai-act-eu-kommission-veroeffentlicht-leitlinien-148086)
- BVDW advises client companies to set AI guidelines for their agencies, e.g. whether product or person images may be AI-generated, and to demand transparency about where agencies use AI — [BVDW PDF](https://www.bvdw.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/BVDW-x-Observatory-KI-Agenturbefragung.pdf)

### Inferences
- Selling points for Claudia in DACH agency groups:
  - strict per-client workspace isolation (client A's data never reaches client B's agent)
  - EU hosting plus a data processing agreement (AVV)
  - audit log of who generated what
  - an "AI used" disclosure flag on outputs, to meet the association guidance and AI Act Art. 50
  - a human-approval step before anything goes external
  - per-client AI policy settings (e.g. "no AI person images for client X")
  - admin dashboard documentation that supports a works-council agreement (Betriebsvereinbarung) — no individual performance surveillance
- Since 81 % of agencies lack ethical guidelines (GWA), a built-in policy template plus team training is a credible add-on service.
- Billing pressure: project/hour-based income dominates (62 %) and AI erodes hours. Features that make AI-delivered value visible (e.g. "report delivered in 10 minutes, X items monitored") support the shift to fixed-price or value models that the BVDW says is emerging.

### Gaps
- No GPRA-specific AI policy document or GPRA member survey was found online.
- The primary DRPR guideline text was not fetched.
- No data found on how many German agencies have a works-council agreement on AI.
- The IHK Munich page on agency remuneration and AI could not be read ([IHK München](https://www.ihk-muenchen.de/ratgeber/digitalisierung/kuenstliche-intelligenz/agenturverguetung-ki/)).

## 4. Survey data on AI adoption in German PR/marketing agencies 2024–2026

### Takeaway
Adoption is close to universal: 98 % of agencies (BVDW 2025) and 59 % of communication-sector firms (IAB 2025, against 25 % of all German firms). Maturity is shallow: 17 % use advanced analytics and 19 % have guidelines (GWA 2025). In 2026 the mood turned defensive: 49 % see AI substitution as a growth threat, and clients demand "AI competence" above everything else (GWA 2026).

### Cited Findings
- BVDW/Observatory 2025: 98 % use GenAI, 69 % increase investment, 29 % have their own models, 54 % customised models; personas: 51 % "AI Innovators", 9 % "AI Skeptics" — [BVDW PDF](https://www.bvdw.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/BVDW-x-Observatory-KI-Agenturbefragung.pdf). The persona labels in the PDF are ambiguously laid out, so the mapping of percentages to personas is uncertain.
- IAB 2025: communication sector 59 % GenAI adoption, against 25 % of all firms — [it-daily](https://www.it-daily.net/?p=175780)
- news aktuell Trendmonitor 2024 (DE/CH, n=327): more than three quarters see AI as an opportunity, 41 % did not yet use it — [Horizont](https://www.horizont.net/schweiz/nachrichten/trendmonitor-wie-pr-spezialisten-in-der-schweiz-und-deutschland-den-einsatz-von-kibewerten-220229)
- GWA AI white paper 2025: 79 % self-described avant-garde vs 17 % using advanced analytics; 19 % with guidelines — [markt-kom](https://www.markt-kom.com/en/?p=231533)
- GWA Frühjahrsmonitor 2026: 49 % AI substitution as a growth inhibitor; 61.1 % of clients increasingly demand AI competence — [GWA PDF](https://www.gwa.de/content/uploads/2026/03/GWA_Fruehjahrsmonitor_2026-1.pdf)
- European Communication Monitor 2024/25 surveyed CCOs of Europe's 300 largest companies with GenAI as one of three focus topics (detailed figures not extracted) — [Akademische Gesellschaft](https://www.akademische-gesellschaft.com/european-communication-monitor-2024/?lang=en)
- International comparison: Muck Rack 2025 found 77 % of PR pros using GenAI and 51 % with an AI policy — [Muck Rack PDF](https://media.muckrack.com/documents/State_of_PR_2025_report.pdf); [PRSA](https://www.prsa.org/article/state-of-public-relations-ai-media-relations-SEPT25)

### Inferences
- The market need has moved from "start using AI" to "make AI systematic, safe and client-visible": shared agents, policies, isolation, workflow integration. This matches Claudia's positioning (per-client and per-team agents, group chats with the agent) better than another single-purpose writing tool.
- Clients asking for AI competence (61 %) means agencies want client-facing AI artefacts they can show: shared client spaces, hosted report pages, live dashboards. This favours Claudia's hosted pages and client group chats.

### Gaps
- No 2025/2026 Bitkom or Horizont study specific to agencies was retrieved. Bitkom figures in the BVDW PDF refer to all companies (9 % GenAI in 2024).
- No HubSpot State of AI 2025/2026 agency figures were retrieved.
- No PR Report / "pressesprecher" agency AI survey for 2025–2026 was found.
- GPRA has no public AI study.
